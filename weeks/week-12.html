Guided Reading:

BrainpopJr. - Compare and Contrast

Warm-Up (3 min): Ask students: What is a theme? Write down responses. Share a definition: A theme is the big idea or lesson in a story.

Lesson (9 min): Read "The Tortoise and the Hare" aloud. Ask students: What lesson can we learn from this story? Guide them to identify the theme (e.g., perseverance, hard work). Write the theme on the board and discuss how the events in the story support it.

Wrap-Up (3 min): Have students share one other story or movie they know and its theme (e.g., "Cinderella" = kindness is rewarded).

Compare themes (e.g., "The Tortoise and the Hare" and "The Lion and the Mouse") - Text in the FOLDER

Warm-Up (3 min): Review: Ask, What is a theme? and list examples from yesterday. Introduce today’s goal: We will compare themes in two stories.

Lesson (9 min): Briefly read "The Lion and the Mouse" aloud. Identify its theme (e.g., kindness is powerful, even small help can make a difference). Compare it with "The Tortoise and the Hare" using a Venn diagram. Differences: themes are perseverance vs. kindness. Similarities: both teach life lessons and involve animals.

Wrap-Up (3 min): Ask: Can two different stories have the same theme? Why or why not?

Writing — Write a full opinion paragraph

Warm-up Debatable Idea: "What is climate change, and should we act on it?" Model turning three reasons into a paragraph: opinion statement, three reasons in order, closing sentence; students write theirs on needs vs wants with starters ("In my opinion... First... Also... Another reason...").

TWR: write the paragraph FROM the outline — topic sentence, one sentence per reason jot, concluding sentence; tick each jot as it becomes a sentence.

Mentor: I Wanna Iguana — read-aloud — tally Alex's reasons and check each has a "because."

Imports & Exports

IXL this week:

Low: Needs and Wants (Gr 1) · High: Needs and Wants (Gr 2) · Extra: Trade and Money (Gr 1)

Stretch: Opportunity Cost (Gr 4) · Understand Overall Supply and Demand (Gr 5)

Review / catch-up — revisit last week's Explorer learning; finish unfinished investigations or SeeSaw posts; preview this week's topic.

Mon · Hook + Investigate

▶ Hook video: ‘Imports & Exports Explained for Kids’ (animated explainer, ~3 min)

(Animated — no short real-footage import/export video found; the real-footage grocery-store supply-chain tour in Week 3 is the real companion clip.)

Vocab: import, export, trade, country.

Hook (kept): “Things come IN to our country (imports) and go OUT (exports).” Brainstorm examples.

Sort printable: Trade in Qatar: Import & Export Sort (Padlet · Week 1)

Objective: Define imports and exports and give an example of each.

Assessment: Sort three brainstormed examples into imports or exports and explain the choice for one.

Differentiation: sort import/export picture cards with an adult modeling the first example / sort independently using the words 'comes in' and 'goes out' as clues / sort independently and explain why an item is an import or export using the term trade

Tue · Apply + Record

Apply — Import/Export Sort & Map It! (kept): decide if each item is an import or export, then point to its country of origin/destination on the globe.

Lesson resource: Import-Export Sort & Map It (Padlet · Week 2)

Record on SeeSaw.

Extra practice: Twinkl: Imports & Exports Sort.

Objective: Classify items as imports or exports and locate their country of origin or destination on a map.

Assessment: Decide whether an item is an import or export and point to the correct country on the globe.

Differentiation: classify with an adult pointing to the country on a labelled globe / classify independently and locate the country with a world map for support / classify and locate the country independently, explaining why the item is an import or export

Wed · Review + Check

Review — Needs vs Wants (kept): compare what we need and want with children in Qatar and another country; quick check.

Resources: BrainPOP Jr: Needs and Wants · Needs vs. Wants (Padlet · Week 2)

Prior-year lesson: Why People Moved to Cities (Padlet · Week 2)

Objective: Compare needs and wants between children in Qatar and another country.

Assessment: Name one need and one want for a child in Qatar and one for a child in another country.

Differentiation: name a need or want using picture cards with adult support / name a need and a want with a sentence starter / compare needs and wants across the two countries independently in full sentences

Extra practice: IXL: What Is Economics? (Grade 5).

Review / catch-up — consolidate Mon–Wed learning; complete recordings/reflections; reteach or extend as needed.
