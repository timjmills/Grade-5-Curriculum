Differentiation:

Reading — Struggling learners: Provide a completed example first. Advanced learners: Have them try summarizing without the SWBST chart.

Guided Reading: Warm-Up (3 min): Play a short YouTube video explaining the SWBST strategy for summarizing. Quick discussion: What does each letter stand for?

Practice (10 min): Read a short historical or science passage (Qatar History fun fact, if possible). Work together to fill in the SWBST chart as a class. Have students summarize in their own words using this structure.

Differentiation:

Reading — Struggling learners: Provide a word bank with alternative words. Advanced learners: Have them paraphrase a full paragraph instead of one sentence.

Guided Reading: Warm-Up (3 min): Show a BrainPOP or YouTube video on Paraphrasing vs. Plagiarism. Discuss: Why can't we copy exactly? (Elicit responses about using your own words).

Practice (10 min): Display a short passage from a mentor text. Model how to paraphrase using synonyms and sentence restructuring. Group activity: Give students a sentence, and they must rewrite it in their own words. Sample sentences tie in UPC (Qatar imports/exports content).

Differentiation:

Reading — Struggling learners: Provide examples of paraphrased and summarized sentences for guidance. Advanced learners: Have them summarize and paraphrase a longer text.

Guided Reading: Warm-Up/Practice (3 min): Quick review: What's the difference between summarizing and paraphrasing? Play a sorting game: Give students examples of sentences and have them decide if they are summaries or paraphrases. Read a short informational passage.

Quick Review/Check: Step 1: Have students summarize the paragraph in one sentence. Step 2: Have students paraphrase one sentence from the paragraph. Compare: How are they different?

Guided Reading: Review/Catch-up. Rotations: See Sunday's Options.

Living Things & Habitats (Existing)

IXL this week:

Low: Food Chains (Gr 1) · High: Identify Roles in Food Chains (Gr 4)

Stretch: How Does Matter Move in Food Chains? (Gr 5) · Interpret Food Webs I (Gr 5)

Entry Point (Day 1 — Sunday):

Entry Point — Endangered / Extinct Game · done on the SUNDAY the unit starts (Day 1)

Entry hook: show photos of animals (oryx, dugong, dodo, dinosaur, tiger…) and ask explorers to sort them into existing · endangered · extinct — what do those words mean, and how can we tell?

Set the mission: each explorer will build a Diorama Biosphere (Week 4) showing an animal in its habitat and how it survives.

Built from the 2025-26 Living Things weekly plans and the EEE task decks (classification, food chains, life cycles, diorama).

Mon · Hook + Investigate

▶ Hook video: ‘Animal Habitats’ — STEM and Beyond (real footage, ~4 min)

Vocab: living thing, habitat, species, biosphere, survive.

Investigate: what do living things need to survive? Match animals to their habitats (desert, ocean, forest).

Additional practice: BrainPOP Jr — Ocean Habitats for a second real habitat model.

Objective: Identify what living things need to survive and match animals to the correct habitat (desert, ocean, forest).

Explorer photos / resources (from last year's plans):

Show baby animal pictures.

Can access this learning through the story of The Very Hungry Caterpillar

Tue · Apply + Record

Apply — Food chains: energy from the Sun → producers → consumers; build a simple food chain for a habitat.

Epic read: ‘Animal Habitats’ / ‘What Is a Food Chain?’ (Epic).

Roles in the chain: name producer, consumer, and decomposer for each link (fungi break down dead matter and recycle it — see BrainPOP — Fungi); sort with the Twinkl — Producers, Consumers & Decomposers sorting activity.

Objective: Build a simple food chain and name the role (producer, consumer, decomposer) of each link.

Wed · Review + Check

Review: living things & their habitats; quick check.

Extra check: IXL Grade 5 Science — Identify roles in food chains.

Objective: Recall the habitats, needs, and food-chain roles of living things covered this week.

Review / catch-up — consolidate Mon–Wed learning; complete recordings/reflections; reteach or extend as needed.

Assessment (Differentiation)

Explore

Entry check: Sort 3 animal photos into existing, endangered or extinct and say why. Low: point and say with picture cards. Mid: use a sentence frame. High: explain one sort with a reason.

Assessment: Sort animal cards into the correct habitat and name one thing that animal needs to survive there.

Differentiation: Sort with a partner and picture cues / sort independently with picture cues / sort independently and explain why the animal needs that habitat.

Assessment: Label a food chain's links as producer, consumer, or decomposer and explain how energy moves from the Sun through the chain.

Differentiation: Label with word bank and picture support / label with word bank only / label and explain each role independently.

Assessment: Answer a quick-check question naming a habitat, a survival need, and a food-chain role from this week.

Differentiation: Answer with sentence starters and a word bank / answer with a word bank only / answer independently in a full sentence.

Review check: Students explain one thing they learned in Explore this week, using a key word from the unit. Low: point and say with a picture. Mid: sentence frame. High: written explanation.
