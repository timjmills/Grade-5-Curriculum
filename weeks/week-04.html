3-day plan (Mon–Wed)

What is Matter? Will It Freeze?

IXL this week:

Low: Classify Matter — Solid, Liquid, Gas (Gr 2) · High: Heating, Cooling & Changes of State (Gr 3)

Stretch: Change-of-State Diagrams (Gr 4) · Change-of-State: Melt, Freeze, Evaporate, Condense (Gr 5)

Entry Point (Day 1 — Sunday):

Entry (Sun · Day 1): HBKU / Texas A&M science visit — spark curiosity about matter.

Resources: Teacher Guide (docx) · Student Observation Log (docx)

Mon · Hook + Investigate: What is Matter?

1. Hook — Watch ‘States of Matter’ — STEM & Beyond in short sections. Introduce and revisit key vocabulary: solid, liquid, gas, particle.

2. Entry task — Collect classroom items and ask students to sort the materials in any way they choose.

3. Discuss — Ask: How did you sort the materials? Is there another way to sort them? Can they be sorted as solids, liquids, and gases?

4. Investigate — Sort 20 classroom items by state of matter. Keep the completed sort for reference later in the unit.

Objective: Identify materials and classify them as solids, liquids, or gases.

Assessment: Name classroom materials and explain which state of matter each one belongs to.

Differentiation: Initial syllable / initial-sound support / independent identification and classification.

5. Preview — Introduce the question for tomorrow’s ‘Will it freeze?’ investigation: Do all liquids freeze in the same way? Briefly show the materials: water, hand sanitizer, and 7Up/lemon drink.

6. Resource activity — Watch Crash Course Kids: What’s Matter? (Epic) and complete the Seesaw States of Matter Sort together as a class.

Objective: Name the three main states of matter.

Assessment: Answer: What is the state of matter for this material?

Differentiation: Name one state with support / name two states with some support / name and classify states independently.

Resources — Reading Comprehension: States of Matter

Explorer photos / resources (from last year's plans):

Practical Experiment: Will It Freeze?

Take water, hand sanitizer, and 7up and they need to hypothesize which will freeze. Record your answers and reasons using our science fair project template recording sheet. Then freeze all three. (Link in folder) - record outcome e.g.:

Tue · Start Experiment + Build Understanding: Will It Freeze?

1. Review — Revisit the three states of matter and ask students to recall one property of a solid, liquid, and gas.

2. Video input — Watch a short section of Master The Art of Glass Blowing with Siemon & Salazar | Showcase Series to connect heating, cooling, and changing materials.

3. Start experiment: Will it freeze? — Set up three labelled samples: water, hand sanitizer, and 7Up/lemon drink. Students predict which liquid will freeze fully, partly freeze, or stay liquid, then record their prediction and reason using the science-fair project template. Place all three samples in the freezer to observe during the next lesson.

4. Guided input — Use States of Matter – How do you know? slides to connect observations to properties such as shape, volume, flow, and particle movement.

5. Reading link — Read or refer to Matter: First Science to reinforce vocabulary and examples of solids, liquids, and gases.

Epic read (Gr 2-4, new): Do You Really Want to Skate on Thin Ice? A Book about States of Matter

6. Quick check — Show students one material or image and ask them to name its state of matter and justify their answer using one property.

Objective: Use properties of matter to classify materials and make a prediction about whether different liquids will freeze.

Assessment: Students record a prediction with a reason and answer: What property helped you identify the state of matter?

Differentiation: Name the state with support / give one property-based reason with some support / predict and justify independently.

Wed · Observe + Explain: Will It Freeze?

1. Reveal results — Take out the three labelled samples from Tuesday’s ‘Will it freeze?’ investigation: water, hand sanitizer, and 7Up/lemon drink. Keep the prediction chart visible so students can compare outcomes with their original ideas.

2. Observe and record — Students record whether each liquid fully froze, partly froze/slushed, or stayed liquid. Encourage precise property words such as hard, runny, sticky, solid, liquid, shape, and flow.

3. Compare predictions — Ask: Which result matched your prediction? Which result surprised you? Which liquid froze the most? Which froze the least?

4. Discuss properties — Ask: What changed? What stayed the same? Which observable properties helped us identify each state of matter?

5. Explain the science — Explain that liquids can freeze, but each liquid has its own freezing point. Water usually freezes easily, while mixtures such as hand sanitizer and 7Up/lemon drink may freeze differently because they contain other substances.

6. Record conclusion — Students add a short conclusion to the science-fair project template: “The liquid that froze the most was ___ because ___.”

7. Science fair connection — Students identify how the same investigation process will support their upcoming science fair project: ask a testable question, make a prediction, record evidence, compare results, and write a conclusion using scientific reasoning.

Objective: Tell the state of matter based on observed properties and explain that different liquids have different freezing points.

Assessment: Record the result for each sample and answer: What properties show its state of matter? Did the result match your prediction?

Differentiation: Name the state with support / describe one property with some support / compare results and explain differences independently.
