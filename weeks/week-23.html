READING

Vocabulary warm-up (rhyme/word-family words for reference): Dates/Rates/Gates/States, Mosque/Kiosk/Disk, Prayer/Carer/Sharer, Star/Far, Moon/Soon, Iftar/Gifter/After.

Guided Reading — Warm-Up: The teacher will begin the lesson by briefly reviewing the reading and comprehension skills that were learned in previous classes. This may include identifying characters, setting, plot, and understanding the sequence of events in a story.

Practice: Reading and comprehension activity — the teacher will provide each group of students with a text that is appropriate for their reading level (short story, chapter from a book, poem, or informational text). Students read the text in their groups, discussing the characters, setting, plot, and main ideas. The teacher circulates, offering support and guidance as needed.

Wrap Up: The teacher will summarize the main points of the lesson, reinforcing the importance of reading comprehension and summarizing as essential skills for understanding a text.

Rotations: SeeSaw — Dictionary Skills · Guide Words · Practice

IXL: G3 Dictionary · G4 Dictionary · G5 Dictionary · G3 Dictionary Entry · G4 Dictionary Entry · G5 Dictionary Entry · G5 Thesaurus

The teacher and students will practice reading comprehension strategies within small groups or as a whole group, identifying skills that help understanding of text:

Writing — Rhyme patterns: AABB and ABAB

Read rhyming nature poems and clap the AABB pattern (lines 1-2 rhyme, 3-4 rhyme); model looking up a rhyme on RhymeZone for a word from the class bank; students build a rhyme word list for their nature topic. Grammar link: nouns — rhyme words often name things in nature.

TWR: scrambled-line activity — rebuild word cards so the rhyme word lands at the end ("the sun goes down / all over town"). Syntax practice disguised as a puzzle.

Mentor: Messy Room by Shel Silverstein (Padlet mentor poems) — clap the rhyme pattern first.

Resources: Writing Poetry for Kids — Ep.6: What is Rhyming? (Padlet Topic 3) — Example Poems — AABB/ABAB (Padlet Topic 3) — RhymeZone (Padlet Topic 2)

IXL: Complete the rhyme (G1) — Label the rhyme scheme (G3) — Rhyming word (G2) — Stretch: G4 — G5

Grammar (Tue) — Run-On Sentences

OBJ: Identify and correct run-on sentences

Packets — Low (K-1): K: Review: Sentences (sentence boundaries), G1: Review — Sentences (teacher models run-ons) · Mid (2-3): G2: Review — Sentence Types (teacher identifies run-ons), G3: Review — Parts of a Sentence/Fragments (extends to run-ons) · High (4-6): G4: L1.18 Run-On Sentences, G4: Review: Fragments/Compound/Run-Ons, G5: L1.26 Complex Sentences (subordination as fix for run-ons), G6: L1.30 Sentence Fragments (extends to run-on correction)

Tiger is endangered it needs our help and we must act now = run-on.

Writing — Write a short rhyming poem (AABB)

Model two rhyming couplets from the rhyme list; students write their own AABB nature poem, checking the pattern by reading aloud and clapping the rhymes.

TWR: combine two short lines where the rhythm bumps — "The bird sings. It sits in the tree." becomes "The bird sings high up in the tree."

Differentiation: Low/Med frames with the rhyme word pre-placed at line ends; High tries ABAB instead.

Resources: RhymeZone (Padlet Topic 2)

3-day plan (Mon–Wed)

Endangered

IXL this week:

Low: Introduction to Adaptations (Gr 2) · High: Introduction to Adaptations (Gr 4)

Stretch: Animal Adaptations: Beaks, Mouths & Necks (Gr 5) · Adaptations: Skins & Body Coverings (Gr 5)

Review / catch-up — revisit last week's Explorer learning; finish unfinished investigations or SeeSaw posts; preview this week's topic.

Mon · Hook + Investigate

▶ Hook video: ‘Endangered Animals’ — STEM and Beyond (real footage, ~5 min)

Vocab: endangered, threat, habitat loss, protect, conservation.

Investigate: what does ‘endangered’ mean and why does it happen? (habitat loss, hunting, pollution).

Extra video: SciShow Kids: Endangered Animals! (~4 min)

Objective: Explain what 'endangered' means and identify causes of endangerment (habitat loss, hunting, pollution).

Assessment: Name one cause of endangerment and explain how it threatens an animal's survival.

Differentiation: Choose the cause from a picture/word bank with support / name the cause with some support / name and explain the cause independently.

Tue · Apply + Record

Apply: research a Gulf/Qatar endangered animal (Arabian oryx (IUCN Red List), hawksbill turtle (WWF), dugong (WWF)) — its habitat & threats; record on SeeSaw.

Objective: Research a real endangered animal (Arabian oryx, hawksbill turtle, or dugong) and identify its habitat and threats.

Assessment: Record the animal's habitat and at least one threat to it on SeeSaw.

Differentiation: Record with a sentence frame and adult support / record with a sentence frame independently / record and explain the threat in own words.

Wed · Review + Check

Review: causes of endangerment; quick check.

Extra support: BrainPOP Jr — Extinct and Endangered Species to recap before the quick check.

Objective: Recall the causes of endangerment and review this week's endangered-animal research.

Assessment: Answer a quick-check question naming a cause of endangerment and one endangered animal studied this week.

Differentiation: Answer using picture cues and a word bank / answer using a word bank only / answer independently in a full sentence.

Review / catch-up — consolidate Mon–Wed learning; complete recordings/reflections; reteach or extend as needed.
