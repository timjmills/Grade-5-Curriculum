READING

Guided Reading: YouTube - 5 W's or Question Words

Warm-Up (3 min) & Lesson (9 min): Begin with the question: "Where are you in this big wide world?" Home Country. Show a globe or a large world map. Ask, "Can you find our host country?" (UAE). Invite a student to locate the host country. Follow up with, "Who are our neighbors? Which other countries do we have connections with?" Discuss how people, places, and connections make the world smaller. Discuss the 5 W questions: Who lives in the host/home countries? What do we know about these places? Where are they located? When did you or your family travel between these places? Why might someone travel or move between these countries? Model comparing and contrasting using a sentence frame: "The host country is [size/geographic feature], while my home country is [size/geographic feature]."

Activity (15 min): Students write a short paragraph comparing the host country and their home country using these questions.

Wrap-Up (3 min): Get students share some ideas from their writing.

Rotations: SeeSaw Digraphs · Digraphs 1 · Digraphs 2

IXL: G2- Digraphs (H) · G2- Spell Digraphs · G3-Digraph-H · G3-Trigraph/Digraph

Google Classroom / Worksheets (Folder) · Boom Cards · RazKids, EPIC, Book Bag

Phonics: Review the "H" Brothers. Explain each and once they have it, explain that sometimes these sounds can be at the end of words too. Use the document in the resource folder to print and have students sort. Ongoing activities throughout the week for reinforcement.

Guided Reading: (e.g., "The Boy Who Cried Wolf" and "The Ant and the Grasshopper") or Text in the FOLDER

Warm-Up (3 min) & Lesson (9 min): Introduction to Researching Skills (15 minutes) Discuss why research is important when learning about different places. Teach strategies for finding credible information; introduce criteria for evaluating credible sources: Author (Who wrote it? Are they an expert?), Publication Date (Is the information current?), Bias (Does the source have a particular opinion or agenda?), Purpose (Is the source informative, persuasive, or entertainment-focused?).

Guided Research Practice (30 minutes): Provide a list of recommended kid-friendly resources (print and online). Assign pairs or small groups to research one of: a geographic feature of the host/home country, a cultural tradition unique to one of the countries, or historical connections between the two countries.

Wrap-Up (3 min): Reflect on the research process: "What was easy or challenging about finding information?" "How can we make sure our information is trustworthy?"

Rotations: See list from Sunday or the differentiation section below.

Phonics: Briefly explain that "r" changes the sound of the vowel it follows (e.g., "ar" in "car," "er" in "her"). Show examples on the board. Read words with r-controlled vowels aloud (e.g., "star," "bird," "fork") and have students repeat after you. Sort words by r-controlled vowel sounds (e.g., ar, er, or) and practice pronunciation. Get students to give examples of r-controlled words from their own experience.

Writing — The counterargument: "Some people think... but I believe..."

Warm-up Debatable Idea: "Should single-use plastics be banned?" Model the frame "Some people think... but I believe... because..." on local vs imported goods; partners rehearse a counterargument for their own opinion, then add it to the draft. Grammar link: conjunctions (however, but) signal the shift.

TWR: BUT is the counterargument machine — the full frame is ONE sentence; one taught conjunction does the whole rhetorical job.

Mentor: Hey, Little Ant — read-aloud — T-chart both sides' reasons: the perfect model for "Some people think..., but..."

Resources: Writing to Persuade: Counterargument — word bank: some people think, but I believe, however

IXL: Use conjunctions (G2) — Coordinating conjunctions (G3) — Stretch: Subordinating (G4) — Coordinating (G5)

Writing — A strong conclusion completes the draft

TWR: SO for the conclusion — draft from "So, we should ___.", then rewrite with a different opener ("That is why..." / "In conclusion...").

Natural Resources & Qatar

IXL this week:

Low: Natural Resources (Gr 1) · High: Natural Resources (Gr 3)

Stretch: Natural Energy Resources: Coal and Wind (Gr 4) · Evaluate Natural Energy Sources (Gr 4)

Review / catch-up — revisit last week's Explorer learning; finish unfinished investigations or SeeSaw posts; preview this week's topic.

Mon · Hook + Investigate

▶ Hook video: ‘Natural Resources for Kids’ — Learn Bright (animated, ~9 min)

Vocab: natural resource, oil, gas, money, jobs.

Investigate (kept): What is Qatar / Doha known for? Oil & gas as natural resources; show an oil-rig image.

Extra video: History of Qatar's Economy (Padlet · Week 1)

Objective: Identify oil and gas as natural resources and explain that Qatar is known for producing them.

Assessment: Name the natural resources Qatar is known for and point to them in the oil-rig image.

Differentiation: name the resource shown in the image with adult support / name both oil and gas as resources using the word bank / name the resources independently and explain why they matter for jobs and money

Extra practice: Twinkl: Renewable vs Non-Renewable Resources Sort.

Objective: Sort natural resources as renewable or non-renewable and classify oil and gas correctly.

Assessment: Sort three resource cards into renewable or non-renewable and explain the choice for oil.

Differentiation: sort with picture cards and an adult modeling the oil example / sort independently using the words renewable and non-renewable / sort independently and explain why oil and gas are non-renewable

Tue · Apply + Record

The Qatar Blockade, 2017 (kept): a short news report — what problems did a blockade cause, and how did Qatar keep getting goods?

Activity sheet: Blockade Act: Sheet & Info — Dynamo (Padlet · Week 1)

Objective: Explain what the 2017 Qatar blockade was and describe one way Qatar kept getting goods during it.

Assessment: Name one problem the blockade caused and one way Qatar solved it.

Differentiation: name a problem or solution using picture support and a sentence frame / name one problem and one solution with a word bank / explain independently, in full sentences, one problem and how Qatar responded

Wed · Review + Check

Review — ‘Was trade fair?’ Quick check.

Prior-year debrief: Trading Game outcomes — was trade fair? (Padlet · Week 4)

Compare Doha & Dukhan (assessment prep): Doha is on the coast (fishing, trade, government); Dukhan has Qatar’s oil fields. Also name Al Wakrah (old fishing village) & Al Khor (pearl diving/fishing). Sentence frame: “Doha has ___. Dukhan has ___.”

Reading (Gr 2-3): 'Oil and Gas: Qatar's Treasure' (Grade 2-3 Readings Pack) · Readings Pack — ADD PADLET LINK

Objective: Compare Doha and Dukhan and describe what each place is known for.

Assessment: Complete the sentence frame 'Doha has ___. Dukhan has ___.' with one accurate detail for each place.

Differentiation: complete the frame with picture cards and an adult prompt / complete the frame independently using the word bank (coast, oil, fishing) / complete the frame independently and add a reason why each place developed that way

Review / catch-up — consolidate Mon–Wed learning; complete recordings/reflections; reteach or extend as needed.
