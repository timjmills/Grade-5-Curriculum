3-day plan (Mon–Wed)

Bridge to the Science Fair

IXL this week:

Low: Support a Hypothesis with Observations (Gr 3) · High: Compare Physical & Chemical Changes (Gr 4, review)

Stretch: Particles of Matter (Gr 5) · Conservation of Matter Using Graphs (Gr 4)

Review / catch-up — revisit last week's Explorer learning; finish unfinished investigations or SeeSaw posts; preview this week's topic.

Mon · Investigate + Practice a Fair Test: Thermal Insulators

1. Review + connect — Recall yesterday's chemical-change work (baking soda + vinegar; reversible vs. irreversible changes). Explain that today's investigation tests a different material property — how well a material holds in heat — using the same fair-test process.

2. Hook — Watch Conductors and Insulators, pausing to ask: Why do we use metal pots and pans? What happens if we leave a metal spoon in a hot pot?

3. Teach — Explain that "thermal" means gaining or losing heat; introduce insulator vs. conductor.

4. Investigate — Pour hot water into paper, styrofoam, plastic, and glass cups. Predict, then check: what happens, how does each feel?

5. Fair-test connection — Point out this is a fair test: only the cup material changes; the water amount and time stay the same. Record on the Science Fair template.

Objective: 5-PS1-2: Measure and compare quantities to provide evidence that materials conduct or insulate heat differently.

Assessment: What lets us know a material is a good insulator?

Differentiation: Can say what makes an insulator with support / name 2 insulators with some support / name more than 2 independently.

Tue · Model the Fair Test: Carbonated Liquids

1. Review — Revisit yesterday's cup results; recall the fair-test rule it modeled: change one thing, keep everything else the same.

2. Connect — Look at two bottles of water — sparkling and still. Taste and describe (small amounts, still first, then sparkly); introduce the word carbonated (forced carbonation vs. fermentation). Ask: how could we fair-test what's really in a fizzy drink?

3. Investigate — Run the carbonated-liquids fair test as a class: mix lemon juice + baking soda, then baking soda + vinegar, then baking soda + water. Only the liquid changes each time — the amount of baking soda, container, and time all stay the same.

4. Hypothesis — Write the hypothesis in IF…THEN…BECAUSE form: “If baking soda is added to lemon juice, vinegar, and water, then the lemon juice and vinegar will not act the same as water, because they are acidic and will react with the baking soda (a base), while water (neutral) will show little or no reaction.”

5. Discuss — Once the gas escapes, can we get it back? This change is irreversible.

Objective: Can carry out a fair test and write a hypothesis in IF…THEN…BECAUSE form.

Assessment: Write a hypothesis for the carbonated-liquids investigation using the sentence frame.

Differentiation: Complete the frame with support / write with a word bank / write independently.

Resources — Vinegar & Bicarbonate of Soda Investigation Pack (Twinkl — replaces broken PDF)

Wed · Practice Write-Up: Using the Science Fair Template

1. Review — Look back at Tuesday's carbonated-liquids investigation and hypothesis.

2. Guided write-up — As a class, complete a full write-up of the carbonated-liquids investigation on the Science Fair template: question, hypothesis, materials, procedure, results (as a simple graph), and conclusion.

3. Discuss — What makes a strong conclusion? Model: "My hypothesis was ___ because ___. The data showed ___." Was the reaction with water different from the reaction with vinegar and lemon juice? Why?

4. Preview — Explain that starting Monday, each student chooses their own investigation question for the Science Fair.

Objective: Can complete every section of the Science Fair template using a shared class investigation as a model.

Assessment: Locate and correctly complete each section of the template (question, hypothesis, materials, procedure, results, conclusion).

Differentiation: Complete sections with sentence starters / with a partner / independently.

Resources — Scientific Process Recording Sheet · ‘The Scientific Method’ – STEM & Beyond

Review / catch-up — consolidate Mon–Wed learning; complete recordings/reflections; reteach or extend as needed.
