READING

Guided Reading: Warm-up (5 min) – Think-Pair-Share: BrainPOP video on Making Inferences. Share a picture card. Ask: "What do you think will happen next?" Students pair up and share their predictions. The Inference Collection - Once Upon a Picture

Practice (7 min): Display a picture with a hidden meaning. Ask: What do you see? What do you already know about this? What can you infer? Use a sentence stem: "I infer that ___ because ___."

Closing (3 min): Students write one inference they made from the picture on a sticky note.

Rotations: SeeSaw Pronoun · Pronoun Practice · Pronoun Practice 1 · Circle the Pronoun

IXL: G2 Pronouns · G5 Pronouns · G4 Pronouns · G5 Pronouns (compound) · G5 Replace Pronouns

Google Classroom/Worksheets: Resources/Worksheets, Boom Cards Pronoun 1 · Pronoun 2, RazKids, EPIC, Book Bag

Guided Reading: Warm-up (5 min) – Mystery Box: Place an item (e.g., sunglasses) inside a box. Give clues. Ask: "What do you infer is inside the box?" Reveal the item and explain how they made the inference.

Practice (7 min): Start and stop throughout the book. Read aloud a short passage from a mentor text (e.g., "The Stranger" by Chris Van Allsburg) or The Arabic Quilt an Immigrant Story. Ask: What do we already know? What clues did the author give? What do we infer?

Closing (3 min): Turn & Talk: Share one inference from the passage.

Guided Reading: Warm-up (5 min) – Video Clip Predictions: Show a short video, Those Shoes. Pause halfway and ask: "What do you predict will happen next?" Discuss why they made that prediction.

Practice (7 min): Read the first few paragraphs of a story (e.g., Owl Moon by Jane Yolen). Pause at key moments and ask: What will happen next? What clues support your prediction? Students write predictions on whiteboards.

Closing (3 min): Students update or revise their predictions based on new details.

Guided Reading: Warm-up (5 min) – Quick Inferencing Game: Display a sentence with missing information, e.g., "Sam was shivering, even though the sun was shining." Ask: What can we infer?

Practice (7 min): Students write a short mystery scene (3–5 sentences) that includes clues that help readers make inferences. Pair up and exchange papers; partners read & infer.

Closing (3 min): A few students share their mystery sentences with the class. Class makes inferences & predictions about them.

Common student error: 'They didn't have no food' = double negative in historical narrative.

Writing — Body section 2 + conclusion

Model turning Category 2 notes into body section 2, then a conclusion that sums up what the reader should remember; students write both, naming their source(s). Grammar link: colons introduce a list of facts or examples.

TWR: the conclusion is one summary sentence — "___ has changed from ___ to ___, so ___." Rehearse orally, write, extend if ready; 'then vs now' lives inside the frame.
