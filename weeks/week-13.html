READING

Guided Reading: BrainpopJr.- Compare and Contrast

Warm-Up (3 min): Ask students: What is a theme? Write down responses. Share a definition: A theme is the big idea or lesson in a story.

Lesson (9 min): Read "The Tortoise and the Hare" aloud. Ask students: What lesson can we learn from this story? Guide them to identify the theme (e.g., perseverance, hard work). Write the theme on the board and discuss how the events in the story support it.

Wrap-Up (3 min): Have students share one other story or movie they know and its theme (e.g., "Cinderella" = kindness is rewarded).

Rotations: SeeSaw Create Simile and Metaphor · Sort Similes/Metaphors · Metaphors · Similes and Metaphors · Character/Setting Review

Guided Reading: (e.g., "The Tortoise and the Hare" and "The Lion and the Mouse") Text in the FOLDER

Warm-Up (3 min): Review: Ask, What is a theme? and list examples from yesterday. Introduce today's goal: We will compare themes in two stories.

Lesson (9 min): Briefly read "The Lion and the Mouse" aloud. Identify its theme (e.g., kindness is powerful, even small help can make a difference). Compare it with "The Tortoise and the Hare" using a Venn diagram. Differences: Themes are perseverance vs. kindness. Similarities: Both teach life lessons and involve animals.

Wrap-Up (3 min): Ask: Can two different stories have the same theme? Why or why not?

Rotations: See list from Sunday or the differentiation section below.

Guided Reading: (e.g., "The Boy Who Cried Wolf" and "The Ant and the Grasshopper") or Text in the FOLDER

Warm-Up (3 min): Review Venn diagram from Day 2. Ask: What do we mean when we contrast something?

Lesson (9 min): Read "The Boy Who Cried Wolf" and discuss its theme (e.g., honesty is important). Contrast it with "The Ant and the Grasshopper" (theme: hard work pays off). Guide students to write or share contrasting sentences using sentence stems (e.g., "The theme of ___ is different from ___ because...").

Wrap-Up (3 min): Ask: Why do you think authors write stories with different themes?

Rotations: See list from Sunday or the differentiation section below.

Writing — Back up a reason with evidence

Warm-up Debatable Idea: "Are self-driving taxis a good or bad idea?" K: antonyms vocabulary placeholder.

Writing — Revise to add evidence throughout

Warm-up Debatable Idea: "Should schools ban mobile phones?" Reread the paragraph aloud ("say it before you fix it"); model adding "For example..." / "I learned that..." to a second reason in the class model; students back up as many reasons as they can.

TWR: combine reason + evidence into one sentence with 'because' or 'since' ("Local food is fresher since it does not travel far.").

Differentiation: Low/Med add evidence to one reason with a starter bank; High backs all three reasons.

Resources: Imports & Exports worksheet (Padlet Wk 2)

3-day plan (Mon–Wed)

Where Goods Come From · Maps

IXL this week:

Low: Read a Map — Cardinal Directions (Gr 2) · High: Read a Map — Cardinal Directions (Gr 4)

Stretch: Identify Lines of Latitude and Longitude (Gr 4) · Use Lines of Latitude and Longitude (Gr 5)

Review / catch-up — revisit last week's Explorer learning; finish unfinished investigations or SeeSaw posts; preview this week's topic.

Mon · Hook + Investigate

▶ Hook video: ‘Take a Tour Through a Grocery Store’ — KidsECON / SC ETV (real footage)

Vocab (kept): map, sea, coast, desert, transport.

Investigate: how do goods move? ships, planes, trucks. Look at maps — where is Qatar; what is near the sea?

Video (new): 'How does a Shipping Container work?' — Maddie's Do You Know (real footage, ~5 min)

Objective: Identify ways goods travel (ship, plane, truck) and locate Qatar and nearby seas on a map.

Assessment: Name one way goods travel and point to Qatar and the sea on a map.

Differentiation: point to Qatar on a labelled map with adult support and name one transport picture / point to Qatar and the sea independently and name two transport methods / locate Qatar and the sea independently and explain why being near the sea helps trade

Tue · Apply + Record

Apply: where does our food / clothing come from? Read the labels and map the countries on a world map.

Twinkl: How Far Did My Food Travel? worksheet.

Reading (Gr 2-3): 'Where Does My T-Shirt Come From?' (Grade 2-3 Readings Pack) · Readings Pack — ADD PADLET LINK

Objective: Read a food or clothing label to find its country of origin and mark that country on a world map.

Assessment: Find the country of origin on one label and mark it correctly on a world map.

Differentiation: find and mark the country with an adult reading the label aloud / find and mark the country independently using a labelled world map / find, mark, and explain independently how far the item travelled to reach Qatar

Wed · Review + Check

Review: why does location matter for trade? Quick check.

Case study: Australia's Top Exports (Padlet · Week 1)

Prior-year lesson: Desert life vs coastal life — Map, Sea, Coast, Desert (Padlet · Week 4)

(Lulu Express / GWC warehouse field trip sits in this week.)

Objective: Explain why a country's location affects how easily it can trade goods.

Assessment: Answer 'why does location matter for trade?' with one reason connected to Qatar's coastline.

Differentiation: answer using a sentence frame and a picture of the coastline / answer independently using the word 'coast' or 'sea' / answer independently in a full sentence explaining the link between location and trade

Review / catch-up — consolidate Mon–Wed learning; complete recordings/reflections; reteach or extend as needed.
