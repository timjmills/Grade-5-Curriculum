1. Curriculum Overview

Grammar is a shared K–6 curriculum: every grade studies the same grammar focus at the same time, choosing from grade-banded packet options (K–2, 3–4, 5–6) drawn from 470 lesson packets stored in the Grammar folder (seven grade subfolders).

Structure: 35 teaching weeks × 2 grammar slots per week = 70 lesson slots across the 7 content units. The two slots run SUNDAY (Slot A — introduce a concept) and TUESDAY (Slot B — extend and apply it). Writing takes Monday & Wednesday; Thursday is catch-up — no new grammar.

Every week also carries IXL practice: a Low (support) and High (on/above level) skill from IXL Grades 1–3, plus two Grade 4–5 stretch links for high students — listed week by week in the full map below and in the weekly plans.

4. The 35-Week Structure

The grammar sequence is organized across six skill strands woven throughout the year. Each strand builds on the one before it, and earlier strands are revisited in the year-end review weeks.

Parts of Speech (Weeks 1–10)

The year opens with the eight parts of speech introduced in a logical order: sentences first, then nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs, pronouns, prepositions, and conjunctions. These form the vocabulary students need for all subsequent grammar work.

Capitalization (Weeks 6–7)

Consecutive weeks of capitalization rules cover all major categories: sentence openings and pronoun I, names and titles and places, and dates/days/months/holidays. These are front-loaded so students can apply them immediately in their writing.

Punctuation (Weeks 7–14)

End marks (periods, question marks, exclamation points) lead into commas (dates, series, compound sentences), apostrophes (possessives then contractions), and advanced marks (quotation marks, colons, semicolons, hyphens, parentheses).

Verb Tense and Usage (Weeks 16–21)

Subject-verb agreement opens Unit 4, followed by irregular verb families, past/present/future tense, helping and linking verbs, tricky usage errors, and comparative adjectives and adverbs. This strand develops verb mastery progressively.

Sentence Structure (Weeks 22–25)

After strong word-level foundations, students analyze sentence parts (subject and predicate), identify and correct fragments and run-ons, and build more complex sentences through combining, compound, and complex sentence structures.

Word Study and Figurative Language (Weeks 27–31)

The penultimate unit (Space) hosts vocabulary and figurative language: homophones, multiple-meaning words, synonyms and antonyms, similes and metaphors, personification, connotation and denotation, and analogies.

Year-End Review and Application (Weeks 32–35)

The final four weeks return to all six strands in a comprehensive review sequence: parts of speech, sentence structure, capitalization/punctuation, verb tense and usage, proofreading, revising, and publishing.

9. Teacher Notes on Grammar Instruction Design

Thematic Alignment Across All Units

Each grammar focus was placed in its unit not just for pedagogical sequence but also for thematic relevance:

Unit 1 — The Amazing Body: Body part nouns (heart, lung, bone) model common nouns. Body action verbs (pump, breathe, digest) model action verbs. Describing the body requires adjectives. The human body provides concrete, visible vocabulary for abstract grammar concepts.

Unit 2 — Changes in Matter: Matter processes use adverbs (melts slowly, evaporates quickly). Science inquiry requires question marks. Lab observations are capitalized sentences with periods. Trade names and scientists' names need proper noun capitalization.

Unit 3 — Global Goods: Trade negotiations are dialogue — perfect for quotation marks. Trade documents require colons, titles, and formal punctuation. Economic plural nouns (nations, imports, prices, taxes) practice plural rules. Possessives describe ownership of goods.

Unit 4 — Our Past and Present: History is written in past tense. Irregular past-tense verbs appear constantly in historical narrative (went, said, gave, fought). Subject-verb agreement matters in formal historical writing. Comparative adjectives describe historical comparisons.

Unit 5 — Existing, Endangered, Extinct: Animal research generates natural subject-predicate sentences. Conservation arguments use complex sentences with because and although. Combining sentences improves the fluency of animal habitat descriptions. Run-ons and fragments are common in field note writing.

Unit 6 — Space: Space vocabulary is rich in homophones and multiple-meaning words (orbit, space, launch, light). Space inspires figurative language: the universe is a vast sea of stars. Connotation matters in scientific language: vast vs. big, ancient vs. old, mysterious vs. unknown.

Unit 7 — Forces, Energy & Disasters: Disaster reporting uses all punctuation rules (dates, quotes, names, dramatic end marks). Disaster descriptions use all verb tenses (past: the earthquake struck; present: teams are searching; future: will rebuild). Publishing a disaster survival guide is the year-end culminating grammar application.

Spiral Design: When to Revisit Earlier Skills

Grammar is not mastered after a single lesson. The curriculum map is designed with deliberate spiraling:

Capitalization is introduced in Weeks 6–7 and revisited every time students write proper nouns, titles, and quoted material in Weeks 11–14 and Week 33.

Pronouns are introduced in Week 4, deepened in Weeks 15 and 20 (contractions and negatives), and reviewed comprehensively at year end.

Verb tense is scaffolded across six weeks in Unit 4 (Weeks 16–21) and reviewed in a dedicated session in Week 33.

Sentence types and sentence structure are introduced in Weeks 1–2, applied in Weeks 22–25, and reviewed in Week 32.

Comma rules are introduced incrementally across Weeks 8–9, extended to compound sentences and dialogue in Weeks 9–11, and reviewed in Week 33.

Thursday is the built-in spiral day. Use it to return to any concept where students need more practice before moving on — the weekly IXL Low links are ready-made spiral material.

Assessment Guidance

Grammar assessment in this model is primarily embedded in student writing. Recommended assessment approaches:

Formative: Observe student writing on Monday/Tuesday (Writing days) immediately after Sunday's grammar lesson. Look for application of the skill taught the day before. IXL's SmartScore on the week's assigned skills gives a second, independent formative read.

Proofreading activities: Use the dedicated proofreading lessons (Week 34 Sunday) as a systematic diagnostic — students who can identify and fix errors demonstrate internalization.

Thursday intervention: Students who struggle with Monday/Tuesday application should receive targeted grammar support on Thursday using the same lesson packet at a lower grade band, or the week's IXL Low skill.

Year-end: The Publishing and Evaluating Writing lessons (Week 35) provide summative evidence of students' ability to apply grammar knowledge in a complete, polished piece of writing.
