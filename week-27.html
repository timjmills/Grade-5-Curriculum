Writing — Story problem and goal

Watch a Story Elements: Problem & Solution video; model adding a problem and goal to the class story-map; students add a problem + goal for their own character, then orally rehearse with a partner. Grammar link: verbs — "wants," "needs," "tries" show the goal.

TWR: the problem is one BUT sentence — "___ wanted ___, but ___." If the 'but' sentence works, the story works.

Mentor: Enemy Pie (Padlet mentor read-alouds) — name the problem and goal, then say them as one "but" sentence.

Resources: Planning Out the Problem worksheet (Padlet student organizers) — Goals & Challenges worksheet (Padlet student organizers)

IXL: Problem and solution in a story (G1) — Order events in a story (G2) — Stretch: Make predictions (G4) — Summarize a story (G5)

Writing — Plan the events: beginning, middle, end

Model planning the class story's events in three parts on the story-map; students plan their own, then orally rehearse telling the whole story to a partner before writing.

TWR: events as dot jots only (2-3 words per event) across beginning-middle-end; the oral retell FROM the jots is the draft-before-the-draft.

Mentor: A Bad Case of Stripes (Padlet mentor read-alouds) — list its beginning-middle-end events on the board as dot jots.

Differentiation: Low/Med event sentence frames per part; High adds a small setback in the middle.

Resources: Story Events List organizer (Padlet student organizers) — co-built rubric (story-map completeness)

3-day plan (Mon–Wed)

Sun · Day, Night & Seasons

IXL this week:

Low: Seasonal Daylight Patterns (Gr 1) · High: Day and Night (Gr 4) · Extra: Shadows (Gr 4)

Stretch: Seasons (Gr 5) · Day and Night (Gr 5)

Review / catch-up — revisit last week's Explorer learning; finish unfinished investigations or SeeSaw posts; preview this week's topic.

Mon · Hook + Investigate

▶ Hook video: National Geographic ‘Sun 101’ (≈4 min)

Vocab: rotation, orbit, axis, tilt, shadow.

Does the Sun Move? Starter (5–10 mins): “Have you ever noticed how your shadow moves during the day? Why do you think that happens?” Show a time-lapse of shadows / a sundial.

Investigate — Shadow Investigation (30 mins): outside, plant a stick (or use playground equipment). Draw around the shadow every 30 mins; measure its length & direction and record the time. “Did the shadow move? Why?” Light & Shadows (Science for Kids) — Padlet.

Additional practice: Twinkl ‘Day and Night Sky Sorting’ activity; read Day and Night by Robin Nelson (Epic) for extra vocabulary support.

Objective: Explain that shadows change position and length across the day because Earth is rotating.

Assessment: Record shadow length/direction at two times and explain why the shadow moved.

Differentiation: record shadow observations with a sentence frame + adult support / record observations independently with a sentence frame / record observations and explain the link to Earth's rotation independently

Tue · Apply + Record

Spin the Earth! (10 mins): use a globe + flashlight to show day & night; put a marker on our home country and watch it go from light to dark.

Objective: Explain that day and night happen because Earth spins on its axis.

Assessment: Use the globe and flashlight to show a marked country moving from light to dark and explain why.

Differentiation: show light-to-dark with the globe + adult support / show and name day/night independently with the globe / show and explain day/night as Earth's rotation independently

Apply — Sunrise & Sunset Investigation (25 mins): plot June vs December data on a bar/line graph and locate the countries on a globe.

Prior-year resources: Earth’s Movement: Rotation vs Revolution PPT (Padlet · Topic 2) · Seasons Simulator (Padlet · Topic 3 · astro.unl.edu)

Use the built-in Doha data: 1 June — sunrise 04:43, sunset 18:21; 1 Dec — sunrise 06:03, sunset 16:42. Compare with London / a far-north country.

‘Earth’s Tilt: The Reason for the Seasons’ (no verified real-footage video found under this exact title; use the video below instead) + Seasons simulator — Padlet.

Watch: ‘Why Do We Have Different Seasons?’ (California Academy of Sciences) to reinforce Earth’s tilt and orbit.

Objective: Use real sunrise/sunset data to explain that seasons are caused by Earth's tilt as it orbits the Sun.

Assessment: Plot June vs December sunrise/sunset times on a graph and explain the difference using Earth's tilt.

Differentiation: plot given data points with adult support / plot the data independently and name the season difference / plot the data and explain the seasonal difference using Earth's tilt independently

Wed · Review + Check

Reflection: “Why does the Sun seem to move? Why do some places have longer summers?” The Earth’s tilt + orbit make day length vary; places near the Equator vary least.

Objective: Explain why day and night happen and why some places have longer summers than others.

Assessment: Quick check: explain day/night and why we have seasons.

Differentiation: explain day/night and seasons with sentence starters + adult support / explain day/night and seasons independently with sentence starters / explain both day/night and seasons in own words independently

Quick check: explain day/night and why we have seasons.

Extra (kept from prior years) — Light Detectives: ‘Light for Kids’ (Learn Bright): mirrors bounce light onto a card without pointing the flashlight at it (opaque/translucent objects); light travels straight until it reflects — the Moon shines because it reflects the Sun’s light. Worksheet: How Light Travels Activity Sheet (Twinkl).

Objective: Explain that light travels in a straight line until it reflects, and that the Moon shines by reflecting the Sun's light.

Assessment: Use a mirror to bounce light onto a card and explain, using the worksheet, why the Moon appears to shine.

Differentiation: bounce light with a mirror + adult modelling and support / bounce light with a mirror and name that it reflects, with some support / bounce light with a mirror and explain reflection and moonlight independently

Showcase: write the Sun & Earth speaking lines.

Review / catch-up — consolidate Mon–Wed learning; complete recordings/reflections; reteach or extend as needed.
